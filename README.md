## Overview

This project forecasts daily mean temperature 7 days ahead (multi-step output, t+1 to t+7) for five weather stations in Canterbury, using daily temperature, wind, rainfall and evaporation records.

| Part | Notebook | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Part_1/src/Preprocessing.ipynb` | Select a common date range, clean and merge the 30 raw files, fill gaps, correlation analysis |
| 2 | `Part_2/src/RNN_Model.ipynb` | Recurrent neural network (RNN) forecasting model |
| 3 | `Part_3/src/GNN_RNN_Model.ipynb` | Graph neural network + RNN model linking the five stations |
| 4 | `Part_4/src/Sensitivity_XAI.ipynb` | Sensitivity analysis and explainability (XAI) |
| 5 | `Part_5/src/Report_Summary.ipynb` | Report summary |

## Dataset

Daily observations (9 am NZ local time) from five stations. Each station has six files: Temperature, Wind, Rain and three evaporation estimates (Penman open-water, Penman PET, Priestley-Taylor PET).

| Station | Network ID | Raw folder | Records available from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Christchurch Aero | 4843 | `data/raw/christchurch_aero/` | 1943 (rain), 1954 (temperature), 1994 (wind) |
| Chch Kyle Street | 24120 | `data/raw/christchurch_kyle_st_ews/` | Nov 2002 |
| Ashburton Aero | 26170 | `data/raw/ashburton_aero_aws/` | Feb 2006 |
| Rangiora | 17244 | `data/raw/rangiora_ews/` | Feb 1999 |
| Hanmer Forest | 11234 | `data/raw/hanmer_forest_ews/` | May 1995 |

*Features (8 per station per day):* `evap_penman_ow`, `evap_penman_pet`, `evap_pt_pet` [mm], `max_temp`, `min_temp`, `mean_temp` [°C], `wind_speed` [m/s], `rainfall` [mm].
*Target:* `mean_temp` [°C], 7 days ahead.

*Modelling period:* 1 March 2006 – 30 September 2025 (7,154 days per station). The start is set by the newest station (Ashburton Aero). The end is set by Chch Kyle Street, whose evaporation records stop on 4 Oct 2025.

**Processed data** (`data/processed/`, generated by Part 1):

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `<station_folder>_daily_clean.csv` | One file per station: `date`, `station`, `station_id` + 8 features, no missing values, original units |
| `all_stations_daily_clean.csv` | All five stations in long format |
| `imputation_log.csv` | For every station-day and feature: observed, or the method used to fill it |

*Train / validation / test split* (chronological, saved in `Part_1/models/split_dates.json`):
train 2006-03-01 → 2019-11-14, validation 2019-11-15 → 2022-10-22, test 2022-10-23 → 2025-09-30.

   * **Part 1 – `Part_1/src/Preprocessing.ipynb`:** run all cells (≈ 10 s).    * **Part 2 – `Part_2/src/RNN_Model.ipynb`:** requires Part 1's outputs. With `RERUN_EXPERIMENTS = False` (default) it loads the saved results of the 36 experiment runs from `Part_2/models/` and runs in under a minute. Set it to `True` to retrain all runs (≈ 10–20 min on a laptop CPU). Saves the final model (`final_model.pt` + `final_model_config.json`), the experiment results, the test comparison table and Figures 2.1–2.6.
   * **Part 3 – `Part_3/src/GNN_RNN_Model.ipynb`:** requires Parts 1 and 2 (it reads Part 2's window, cell type and final model for the comparison). With `RERUN_EXPERIMENTS = False` (default) it loads the 15 saved experiment runs from `Part_3/models/` and runs in about a minute. Set it to `True` to retrain (≈ 45–60 min on a laptop CPU). Saves the final GNN+RNN model and configuration, the comparison tables and Figures 3.1–3.7.
   * **Part 4 – `Part_4/src/Sensitivity_XAI.ipynb`:** requires Parts 1–3. With `RERUN_EXPERIMENTS = False` (default) it re-uses the 24 saved sensitivity runs in `Part_4/models/sensitivity_runs/`; if interrupted while retraining, re-running it resumes from the completed runs. Full retraining takes ≈ 60–75 min on a laptop CPU; the XAI section takes a few minutes. No extra XAI package is needed: integrated gradients, occlusion and permutation importance are implemented in PyTorch.
   * **Part 5 – `Part_5/src/Report_Summary.ipynb`:** Markdown only (no code to run). It synthesises Parts 1–4 and gives operational recommendations and a reflection.

## Key findings

### Part 1 – Preprocessing

* **Data quality is high.** Just 1.6 % of values in the modelling period were missing, and only one value failed a  validity check (a mean temperature above the daily maximum). Extreme wind and rain days were kept: they coincide across stations, so they are real weather events.
* **Missing values** were filled in tiers:
  * mean from (max + min)/2 where only the mean was missing;
  * time interpolation for gaps of ≤ 3 days;
  * regression from the most correlated neighbouring station for longer gaps and all rainfall gaps. Rainfall uses a zero-intercept ratio so dry days stay dry. The longest gap was 393 days of Hanmer Forest evaporation. Hold-out error of the filled values is about 0.2–0.5 mm for PET evaporation and 0.7–1.6 °C for mean temperature.
* **Strongest predictors of mean temperature 7 days ahead:** Priestley-Taylor PET (r ≈ 0.64), Penman PET and current mean temperature (≈ 0.60), then min/max temperature (≈ 0.55). Rainfall has no linear relationship at t+7.
* **Most of this signal is seasonal.** With the annual cycle removed, today's temperature anomaly predicts tomorrow's (r ≈ 0.45), but the relationship fades to ≈ 0 by t+4. Forecast skill beyond 2–3 days will mainly  come from the seasonal cycle, so models in Part 2 should be compared against a seasonal-climatology baseline.
* **Stations are strongly linked** (mean temperature r = 0.92–0.98 between stations), which motivates the multi-station graph model in Part 3.

### Part 2 – RNN model (trained at Christchurch Aero, tested at all five stations)

* **Experiments:** simple RNN, LSTM and GRU × input windows of 7, 14, 30 and 60 days, 3 seeds each, selected on validation RMSE. Window length mattered most (30–60 days ≈ 3.11 °C vs 7 days ≈ 3.20 °C), while cell type made almost no difference (≤ 0.01 °C).
* **Final model:** GRU, 60-day window, 2 × 64 units (validation RMSE 3.10 °C).
* **Test performance (RMSE, t+1…t+7 pooled):**

  | Station | Role | RMSE (°C) | Bias (°C) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Christchurch Aero | source | 3.00 | +0.24 |
  | Chch Kyle Street | test only | 2.85 | 0.00 |
  | Ashburton Aero | test only | 3.14 | +0.31 |
  | Rangiora | test only | 2.97 | +0.62 |
  | Hanmer Forest | test only | 3.72 | +1.31 |

* The model is far better than persistence everywhere and slightly better than seasonal climatology at the lowland stations. Its advantage is at 1–2 days ahead; from day 3 it effectively forecasts the seasonal average.
* Transfer without retraining works where the climate resembles Christchurch's, but not at inland Hanmer Forest, where forecasts are too warm (+2.1 °C in winter).

### Part 3 – GNN + RNN (all five stations forecast together)

* **Graph:** 5 nodes, fully connected and undirected. Haversine distances are 7–174 km; edge weights use a Gaussian kernel (σ = 77.8 km) and are row-normalised.
* **Final model:** a shared GRU (2 × 64, 60-day window) encodes each station's history first, then two graph layers exchange information between stations (temporal → spatial), and a shared head outputs 7 days per station. This configuration was chosen on validation data from 5 configurations × 3 seeds, including a no-graph ablation.
* **Test RMSE (pooled over t+1…t+7), RNN (Part 2) → GNN+RNN:**

  | Station | RNN (°C) | GNN+RNN (°C) | Change |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Christchurch Aero | 3.00 | 3.03 | +0.7 % |
  | Chch Kyle Street | 2.85 | 2.88 | +1.1 % |
  | Ashburton Aero | 3.14 | 3.13 | −0.4 % |
  | Rangiora | 2.97 | 2.92 | −1.4 % |
  | Hanmer Forest | 3.72 | 3.46 | −6.9 % |

* The GNN+RNN has small biases at every station and beats each station's climatology. Hanmer Forest's warm bias falls from +1.31 to +0.21°C.
* **The no-graph ablation performs as well as the GNN+RNN on the test set.** The improvement over Part 2 therefore comes from training one model on all five stations with per-station scaling, not from the spatial edges. The stations are highly correlated, and at daily resolution weather reaches all of them on the same day, so neighbours add little information.

### Part 4 – Sensitivity analysis and explainable AI

* **Feature removal (re-trained, 3 seeds):** removing the evaporation features worsens test RMSE by +0.15°C (+4.8%, mostly at t+7). Wind removal has no effect. Rainfall removal has no effect overall but worsens next-day (t+1) forecasts by +0.09°C.
* **Station removal:** removing any one station changes accuracy elsewhere by ≤ 0.015°C (within seed noise). But if a station's own sensors fail at forecast time, its forecast error rises by 1.1–1.4°C, because the neighbours do not compensate.
* **XAI (integrated gradients, occlusion, permutation importance):**
  * The three most influential inputs are **Priestley-Taylor PET, mean temperature and Penman PET**.
  * PET acts as the model's seasonal clock.
  * Rainfall drives correct forecasts of cold changes (−3.1°C in the cold-day case).
  * The largest error (a sudden 28°C nor'wester) had no precursor in the inputs.
* **Perturbation sensitivity** (each input shifted by ±0.5 and ±1 σ, no retraining): the forecast is most sensitive to mean temperature and Priestley-Taylor PET at t+1 (≈ 1.4°C per σ), and to the PET features at t+7 (1.5–1.7°C per σ). Rain cools tomorrow's forecast but not next week's.
* **Agreement and conflict:** all evidence agrees that evaporation matters most.
  * **Wind:** the model responds to wind (−0.75°C per σ), but retraining without it loses nothing, so its information is redundant.
  * **Rainfall:** correlation and global importance under-rate its short-lived, event-driven value.
  * **Neighbouring stations:** attribution to them is high, but they are redundant for accuracy.

### Part 5 – Synthesis

* **Both models beat persistence but add little to the seasonal average beyond 1–2 days.** Their typical error (≈ 3°C) is the same size as the 2–5°C day-to-day changes that matter for decisions, and they smooth those changes away. The limit is the information in the inputs, not the model.
* **Recommended approach:** a single multi-station model with per-station scaling, used as 1–2-day guidance, with intervals (≈ ±5 °C at t+1, ±6 °C at t+7) and climatology shown alongside. The graph component is not justified by accuracy with the current five daily-resolution stations.
* **Data-quality priorities:**
  * PET evaporation, then mean/min temperature and rainfall;
  * each station's own data feed;
  * Chch Kyle Street's evaporation record, which ended in Oct 2025.
* **The most promising improvements:**
  * upstream stations further afield (e.g. Invercargill, Dunedin, Greymouth);
  * hourly data;
  * pressure, humidity, radiation and wind direction, sunshine hours.
